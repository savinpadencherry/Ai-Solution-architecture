🧩 Modularity — Real Family vs Fake Family

Everyone agrees code should be “modular.” Almost nobody agrees what that means. Two skills in this lesson: how much to split (granularity), and telling a real family from a fake one (cohesion).

Part 2 · The Cohesion Ladder P1.3.2

Cohesion asks one question about a module: do these functions belong together for a real reason, or were they just dumped in the same file? Computer science ranked the 7 levels of “belonging” from best to worst:

🥇Functionalevery part serves one single job — a real family
🥈Sequentialan assembly line — output of one part feeds the next
🥉Communicationalparts work on the same data but do unrelated things with it
4Proceduralno data flows between them — but they must run in an order
5Temporalbonded by a moment in time (“we all run at startup”), not a purpose
6Logicalcategory-brothers (“all about strings”) who are otherwise strangers
💀Coincidentalthe junk drawer — random stuff in one file for no reason at all
Why you should care: splitting a truly cohesive module only creates coupling between the halves. And a junk-drawer module is where codebases rot first. Cohesion is the difference between “split” and “shatter.”
