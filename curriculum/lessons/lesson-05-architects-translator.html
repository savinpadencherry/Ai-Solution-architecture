🗣️ The Architect's Translator

Architects speak two languages. Business says “customers must trust us” — engineering needs “99.95% uptime, zero payment loss, full audit trail.” This lesson trains the translation — with two playable simulations.

Part 1 · Queue vs Topic — replay the fight P1.2.4 · consolidation

The auction system: Bid Producer must deliver every bid to three services. You control two things: the messaging shape, and a button that adds a 4th service. Watch what each shape costs.

existing wiring change required (pain) free addition

📬 Queues give / cost

  • Private lines — wiretapping trips an alarm
  • Each consumer gets its own data contract
  • Per-queue monitoring → autoscaling
  • Every new listener = touch the producer

📢 Topic gives / costs

  • Add listeners forever, producer untouched
  • Producer fully decoupled from consumers
  • Anyone can silently subscribe = wiretap risk
  • Everyone gets the same payload; no per-consumer contracts

Part 2 · The Translator — business speak → engineering targets P1.2.5 · new

Stakeholders never say “we need 99.95% availability.” They say human things. Click the business statements you’d hear in a real meeting — watch them translate into named architecture characteristics, each with a number attached. Numbers are the whole trick: a characteristic without a number can’t be designed, tested, or defended.

⬇️ Your selection, translated into architecture characteristics with measurable targets:
The 3–5 rule: real systems optimize for 3 to 5 characteristics, not 15. Every characteristic you add fights the others (security vs speed, scale vs simplicity). Picking the few that matter — and rejecting the rest, on record — is the actual architecture decision.
