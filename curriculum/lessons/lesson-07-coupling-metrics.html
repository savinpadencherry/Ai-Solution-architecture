📈 Coupling Metrics — the Zone of Pain Map

Lesson 06 taught your gut to feel cohesion. This lesson gives your gut numbers: architects measure how much a component leans on others, and whether its position on the map is healthy — or a slow-motion injury.

Part 1 · The Stability See-Saw

Two directions of dependency, two pieces of jargon: afferent = arrows coming IN (“who leans on me”), efferent = arrows going OUT (“who I lean on”). Press the buttons and watch the see-saw. 🍽️ Analogy: the kitchen that every waiter depends on — vs the specials board that chases the market.

Instability  I = efferent ÷ (afferent + efferent) = —
⚖️ Balanced-ish
Dependencies flow both ways at a moderate rate.
The twist most people miss: neither pole is “bad.” Stability is a role, not a score. Rock-solid foundations should be boring; skins and screens should be restless. Trouble starts when a component sits at the wrong pole for its job.

Part 2 · The Map — drag your component the centerpiece

Architects plot every component on two axes: A = how abstract (contracts & interfaces, vs real working code) and I = instability from Part 1. The healthy ones live near the main-sequence diagonal: as abstract as its instability demands. Drag the dot (or tap anywhere) — the map diagnoses where you landed.

A = abstract elements ÷ total elements = 0.50
I = efferent ÷ (afferent + efferent) = 0.50
Distance from main sequence  D′ = |A + I − 1| = 0.00
🟢 On the healthy diagonal
As abstract as its instability demands. Nothing to fix.

💡 Try these two drags: bottom-left corner (stable and concrete — the pain trap) and top-right corner (abstract and restless — the vanity trap). Then find the diagonal.
